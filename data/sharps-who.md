# Who the real operators were

Pulled: 2026-08-19.
Purpose: separate documented operators from TV touts for Brett Cunningham's 2026 NFL desk.
Rule: if a win rate or bankroll is not in a cited source, it is not here. Contest ATS records are public one-season samples, not lifetime edges. Self-claims are labeled as such.

How to read this
- Operator: built a model, an info net, a shop, or a get-down machine, and was written about as the person books feared.
- Other side: the oddsmaker who priced the NFL line those operators attacked.
- Writer / trader: published the process. Useful. Not "greatest ever."
- Contest winner: one public ATS season in a 5-picks-a-week format. Documented. Not a syndicate.
- Tout / entertainer: sold picks, sold a persona, or sold a line on television. Ignore for process.

## Billy Walters and The Computer Group — CONFIRM (operator)

Who: Michael Kent (math / software), Dr. Ivan Mindlin (surgeon, public face, get-down), Billy Walters (joined 1983; money mover, later his own syndicate). Also named in period reporting: Stanley Tomchin, Jimmy Evart / Jimmy Evarts (New York placement). Kent died 2018; posthumously inducted into the Sports Gambling Hall of Fame, 2024.

Sport / era: US sports, especially college football and NFL, plus college basketball. Peak syndicate ~1980–1985. Walters continued after the group broke up; he kept working with Kent until Kent retired in 2000.

What they actually did: Kent wrote one of the first computer programs that spat out a number (predicted spread). The group compared that number to the posted Vegas line and bet the gap. Mindlin and later Walters built a national runner / bookie network so the same side could be bet in many shops before the line moved. ESPN, citing people who worked around the operation, describes a later Walters method of sometimes betting the first side to push a number, then hammering the other side at the better price. That is steam + shop + disguise, not "picks."

Documented vs folklore:
- Documented: ESPN's 2015 feature, drawing on interviews and legal records, says the 1985 FBI raid on the Computer Group showed the syndicate won 60.3 percent of its college football picks in one season. Same piece: Walters was hired in 1983 to exploit weak lines and move money. Review-Journal, 2024: Walters said there would have been no Computer Group without Kent's software, and that Kent did the original handicapping.
- Documented legal history (not a betting edge): Walters was convicted in 2017 of conspiracy, securities fraud, and wire fraud in a Dean Foods insider-trading case (sports betting was not the charge). AP: President Trump commuted the prison sentence on 20 Jan 2021; the conviction was not erased.
- Folklore / unlabeled claims: "hundreds of millions," "only one losing season in 39 years," and later weekly win-rate figures appear as boasts or as "sources told ESPN." Not treated as fact here.

Desk takeaway: the template is still the template. Make a number. Bet only when the market is wrong by enough. Shop. Get down without becoming the line.

Source: https://www.espn.com/espn/feature/story/_/id/12280555/how-billy-walters-became-sports-most-successful-controversial-bettor

## Bob Stoll ("The Professor") — PARTIAL CONFIRM; nickname discarded

Who: Bob Stoll, UC Berkeley statistics background, founder of Dr. Bob Sports (1987). Documented handle is "Dr. Bob," not "The Professor." Modern pick sites using "The Professor" are other people. Discard that nickname for him.

Sport / era: College football first; NFL and basketball also. Mid-1980s to present. Commercial handicapper (sold picks) who also built math models.

What he actually did: Started as a situational-angle handicapper, then built score-projection models after a losing 2003 football season. WSJ, Jan 2007 ("The Man Who Shook Up Vegas"): his college-football recommendations over the prior three seasons were winners ATS 63 percent of the time by records "tracked by dozens of bettors and bookmakers"; 2005 listed as 51-21. That is a recommendation record, not an audited bankroll. CNBC, 2013: Stoll said the old 60-percent-plus era is gone because syndicate models now set the line, and he had to deviate from his own machine on things models miss. He sold those plays (Gold Sheet, Las Vegas Sports News column "The Doctor Is In," 900-number era, then a subscription site). MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference speaker, 2012.

Documented vs folklore: Serious press exists (WSJ, CNBC, ESPN E:60, Sloan). He is not a secret syndicate. He is a documented modeler who chose the tout channel. Do not import his site's later self-reported NFL percentages.

Desk takeaway: early quantitative CFB/NFL work is real. The business model (sell the card) is not the operator model.

Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2013/09/01/the-new-vegas-bookie-is-a-cyborg.html

## Lem Banker — CONFIRM (pre-computer operator; later also a columnist)

Who: Lester "Lem" Banker (1927–2020). Bronx-born, Las Vegas from 1959. Never drew a regular paycheck, per Review-Journal.

Sport / era: US sports, NFL Super Bowls in particular, 1960s–1990s prime. Reduced to small bets late in life because lines equalized.

What he actually did: Power ratings on legal pads. Nationwide runners. A man in the New York Times building paid to read out-of-town papers so Banker could bet a Seattle number that New York had not seen. Shop, not model. Arne Lang (Sports Betting and Bookmaking) to the RJ: he was "a wiseguy before they invented the term." Profiled in Larry Merchant's 1973 The National Football Lottery. Wrote Lem Banker's Book of Sports Betting (1986). Also wrote syndicated handicapping columns — that is a media job on top of the betting.

Documented vs folklore:
- Documented: RJ obituary. Newspaper Super Bowl ATS picks: 20 of the first 23, including 13 straight from 1973 to 1985. That is a published-pick streak, not a lifetime P&L.
- Folklore: "never lost to a bookmaker" and similar quotes. Treat as color.

Desk takeaway: before computers, the edge was information latency + line shopping. That edge is mostly dead on NFL sides. The habit (bet the number, not the team) is not.

Source: https://www.reviewjournal.com/sports/betting/legendary-las-vegas-pro-sports-bettor-lem-banker-dies-at-93-2187275/

## Jimmy "The Greek" Snyder — DISCARD as operator; CONFIRM as oddsmaker / TV tout

Who: James George Snyder Sr., born Dimetrios Georgios Synodinos (1918–1996). Steubenville, Ohio → Las Vegas 1950s.

Sport / era: NFL television, 1976–1988 on CBS The NFL Today. Earlier: sold a line, wrote a Las Vegas Sun odds column that was widely reprinted.

What he actually did: Made and sold odds; then predicted scores on network TV so viewers could back into a spread pick without CBS saying "bet." Encyclopedia.com: he brought gambling onto American television. He was not documented as a Computer Group-style operator who beat books with a model and a runner net. 1962: convicted of interstate transportation of bets and wagering information; fined $50,000; later pardoned by President Ford (1974). Fired by CBS, 16 Jan 1988, after on-camera remarks about Black athletes and slavery.

Documented vs folklore: The 1948 Truman bet appears in his autobiography and later interviews. Not independently audited here; omit the dollar figure. Do not call him one of the greatest sports bettors.

Desk takeaway: he is why the public thinks "handicapper = TV guy with a score prediction." That is the opposite of this desk.

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Snyder_(sports_commentator)

## SuperContest / Circa Million — CONFIRM the contests; do not promote winners to "greatest ever"

What these are: public NFL ATS contests. Entrants pick five games a week against that shop's locked number. A hot 18-week sample can pay $1 million and still be variance. Use them as a public leaderboard, not a Hall of Fame.

Westgate (Hilton) SuperContest — documented winners, Review-Journal:
- Steve Fezzik (legal name Steve Fenic in later press): only back-to-back SuperContest winner, 2008 and 2009. RJ, 7 Jan 2010 and 2010 Youmans column: 2008 field 350, 2009 field 328. Chicago Sun-Times, 24 Jan 2026: those wins paid $220,000 and $210,000. Former actuary, Fellow of the Society of Actuaries. Also sells picks — hybrid contest pro / commercial handicapper.
- Briceton Jamar Branch, alias Granny's Boy: 2017 SuperContest, 58-22-5, $1.3 million (RJ, 7 Jun 2018).
- Ryan LeGates, alias SA247LLC: 2022 SuperContest, 60-29-1, first-place $197,200, total contest haul $311,708.90 (RJ).
- Bryan Boren, alias Biffs Almanac: 2025 SuperContest, 61-28-2, $231,630 overall plus in-season cash to $321,630 (RJ).

Circa Sports Million — documented $1 million first-place winners, RJ / Circa:
- 2019: Isaac Meier, alias Booty Blockers, 56-25-3 (Survivor Sweat contest history).
- 2020: Dakota Reed, 56-27-2 (same).
- 2021: Tony Gordon, alias DURBIFY, 63-27; won on tiebreakers vs Jeff Petch and Jim Griffin (VSiN / Survivor Sweat).
- 2022: CHIEF-3 — John Miller, Brian Koestering, Zachary Zoltowski, Anthony Coratti, 59-26-5 (RJ, 11 Jan 2023).

Repeat names that show up as working handicappers, not one-hit aliases: Steve Fezzik, Tony Gordon, Jeff Petch (2021 Circa runner-up; 2023 Circa Friday Football Invitational champion, per VSiN invitational preview).

Desk takeaway: a contest % is not a closing-line-value book. Fezzik is the only name here with a multi-year, multi-outlet paper trail as an NFL contest specialist.

## Ed Miller and Matthew Davidow — CONFIRM (writers / traders, not "greatest ever")

Who: Ed Miller (poker author first). Matthew Davidow (book bio: sports modeler, co-founded two private sports-analytics firms). Co-authors of The Logic of Sports Betting (2019) and Interception (2023). Co-founded Deck Prism Sports, an in-play odds / sportsbook startup (RJ, 2020).

Sport / era: Modern US legal market, 2000s–2020s. Process book, not an NFL-only syndicate profile.

What they actually did / taught (RJ interview, 13 Jun 2020):
- Attack surface: the menu (sides, totals, first half, props) is the player's edge, because books cannot price every derivative when the main moves.
- Bet angles (quantifiable, causal, not in the number), not newspaper trends.
- Live bets: wait for a timeout; broadcast delay is a tax.
- Parlays are not evil if the legs are +EV; they are a multiplier of whatever you already are.

Documented vs folklore: No sourced lifetime NFL win rate or bankroll. Do not invent one. Their value is the market map: retail books limit winners; sharp books are the close; CLV is the grade.

Desk takeaway: this is the modern operating manual. Judge the $150 straights on CLV, not on Sunday night P&L.

Source: https://www.reviewjournal.com/sports/betting/logic-of-sports-betting-authors-offer-some-tips-2052527/

## Stanford Wong (John Ferguson) — CONFIRM (author / advantage-play math; not an NFL syndicate)

Who: John Ferguson, writes as Stanford Wong. Doctorate in finance, Stanford. Professional Blackjack (1975). Sharp Sports Betting (Pi Yee Press, 2001). LAT, 24 Jul 2003: he wrote the sports book because the existing literature was "really inadequate" and working sports bettors were "very secretive." Also ran a paid Sharp Sports Betting NFL discussion site.

Sport / era: Blackjack first (1960s–1990s). Sports-betting book 2001; NFL-heavy math (spreads vs moneylines, parlays, teasers, props, Poisson tables, push frequencies).

What he actually did: Translated casino advantage-play thinking to sports: vig math, 52.38 percent break-even at -110, key-number charts, why teasers and parlays are usually minus-EV. He did not publish a documented NFL syndicate P&L.

Documented vs folklore: No sourced lifetime sports win rate here. The book is a primer, not a trophy.

Desk takeaway: still the cleanest published NFL math. Key numbers and push tables belong on the wall. His "people who bet sports were secretive" line is why this file exists.

Source: https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2003-jul-24-fi-golden24-story.html

## Joseph Buchdahl / Pinnacle trading literature — CONFIRM (analyst; soccer-heavy; process transfers)

Who: Joseph Buchdahl, Football-Data.co.uk / Tennis-Data.co.uk since ~2001. Books: Fixed Odds Sports Betting (2003), How to Find a Black Cat in a Coal Cellar (2013, tipster testing), Squares & Sharps, Suckers & Sharks, Monte Carlo or Bust. Regular Pinnacle Betting Resources contributor (60+ pieces, per his publisher).

Sport / era: Mostly soccer / tennis odds research, 2000s–2020s. Not an NFL operator.

What he actually did: Showed, with Pinnacle opening-vs-closing data, that the close is a tight probability estimate and that the ratio of the price you took to the close predicts long-run yield better than a short win-loss record. His own site: "If you're wondering whether your betting record is showing evidence of skill, don't look at the size of the bank balance… find out whether you're beating the closing price" of an efficient book (Pinnacle) or an exchange (Betfair). Also the standard demolition of Martingale and of unverified tipster ads.

Documented vs folklore: He is a researcher and data vendor, not a documented NFL sharp. Soccer sample sizes are why the papers exist. The CLV idea is what transfers.

Desk takeaway: the 2026 ledger already says this. Profit is variance. CLV of straights is the grade.

Source: https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/betting-strategy/using-the-closing-line-to-test-your-skill-in-betting/7e6jwjm5ykejuwkq

## Tony Bloom / Starlizard — CONFIRM (soccer; not NFL)

Who: Tony Bloom, Brighton & Hove Albion owner. Founded Starlizard (2006), a London data / modeling firm. He is the primary client of his own models. Business Insider, 2016: statistical models aimed at Asian-handicap football markets; staff invited into his syndicate.

Sport / era: Soccer (football), 2000s–2020s. Also racehorse owner. Not an NFL shop.

What is documented vs alleged: Starlizard as a data company and Bloom as a long-running soccer bettor is documented (BI; Wikipedia summary of the firm). A 2025 High Court claim by former associate Ryan Dudfield alleged the syndicate makes about £600 million a year; Bloom's lawyers called that figure an "irrelevant exaggeration" (Gambling Insider, Jan 2026 reporting on the defence). Do not treat the £600m figure as fact. FA dispensation to stay in football and keep betting, with restrictions on his own clubs / certain competitions, is widely reported.

What transfers: a standing quant shop, Asian lines, get-down through third-party accounts. The sport does not transfer. Do not copy soccer methods onto NFL key numbers.

Source: https://www.businessinsider.com/inside-story-star-lizard-tony-bloom-2016-2

## Zeljko Ranogajec and Alan Woods — CONFIRM (horses; not NFL)

Who: Alan Woods (1945–2008), Australian mathematician, blackjack then Hong Kong horses; worked with Bill Benter, later with Ranogajec. Zeljko Ranogajec (also "the Joker," later "John Wilson"), Australian advantage player, blackjack → horses / totes worldwide; long associated with David Walsh and a syndicate reporters called the Bankroll / Punters Club.

Sport / era: Horse racing (Hong Kong Jockey Club, Australian totes, later global). 1980s–present for Ranogajec; Woods through 2008.

What they actually did (what is sourced, not guessed):
- Woods: after blackjack, Hong Kong horses with Bob Moore and Bill Benter; model on track, form, weather. Wikipedia (citing contemporary racing press): model returned about $100,000 in the 1987 season, then Woods and Benter split. Later Manila / Ranogajec era. 2006–07: his syndicate reported as about 2 percent of HKJC turnover.
- Ranogajec: high-liquidity pools, small edges, huge last-minute volume, rebate deals with totes. 2008 court: he told a federal magistrate the syndicate turned over about $1 billion a year (Brisbane Times, 2025, recounting that testimony). News.com.au / SMH industry sources have described him as Tabcorp's largest punter. Those turnover figures are industry estimates or his own court guess, not an audited profit.

What transfers to NFL: residual vs the public price; size only where the pool can take it; rebates / reduced juice matter as much as the pick; secrecy. What does not transfer: pari-mutuel pool smash, tote rebates, last-second flooding of a horse pool.

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Woods_(gambler)

## Bill Benter — CONFIRM (horses; the paper is the source)

Who: William "Bill" Benter. Pittsburgh blackjack counter → Hong Kong Jockey Club. Partnered with Alan Woods in the mid-1980s, then ran his own syndicate (Paul Coladonato named in Bloomberg as a later associate).

Sport / era: Hong Kong horse racing, mid-1980s through ~2001 retirement from the first run. Not NFL.

What he actually did: 1994 paper, "Computer Based Horse Race Handicapping and Wagering Systems: A Report" (in Efficiency of Racetrack Betting Markets). Multinomial logit win probabilities from fundamentals, then a second step that blends those probabilities with the public tote odds. Bet the residual. Fractional Kelly. He reports significant positive results over five years of live betting and treats that as evidence the tote was inefficient. Bloomberg, 3 May 2018 ("The Gambler Who Cracked the Horse-Racing Code"): first long interview; describes a computer-driven HK operation at scale, including a Triple Trio hit. Bloomberg's "close to a billion dollars" framing is journalism, not a tax return. Use the paper, not the headline.

What transfers: (1) make your own probability, (2) do not ignore the market — the public number is a feature, (3) bet only the leftover, (4) size by edge, not by gut. That is the same sentence as Kent/Walters, in a different sport.

Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code

## The other side: Vegas oddsmakers

### Bob Martin — CONFIRM (linemaker, 1960s–early 1980s)

America's opening number. Churchill Downs sportsbook on the Strip, then Union Plaza downtown. Sports Handle: Monday-morning football openers that illegal books nationwide copied. His edge was a phone graph of the biggest bookmakers in New York and everywhere else — he knew what number would draw two-way action. Convicted 1982 for transmitting betting information across state lines (Wire Act); short prison term. That hole is how Roxy's consulting business was born. Inaugural Sports Gambling Hall of Fame, 2023.

Source: https://sportshandle.com/remembering-bob-martin-las-vegas-oddsmaker/

### Michael "Roxy" Roxborough — CONFIRM (bettor, then the national line)

Before he made lines he beat Cal Neva on baseball totals. Chris Andrews started buying those totals, then all sports. Roxy founded Las Vegas Sports Consultants (1982–1999); RJ: provided lines to more than 90 percent of Nevada books. ESPN Chalk: he spent half his time dragging books into the computer age. RJ: he has said Billy Walters and the Computer Group forced books onto formulas. Co-founded "America's Line" (128 newspapers). Inaugural SGHOF.

Desk takeaway: the person who invented the modern opening number did it because a model-and-runner syndicate was eating the old number.

Source: https://www.reviewjournal.com/sports/betting/roxy-roxboroughs-rise-from-bettor-to-oddsmaker-to-hall-of-famer-2885678/

### Chris Andrews — CONFIRM (book, not bettor)

South Point sportsbook director. Uncle: Jack Franzi ("Pittsburgh Jack"). CDC Gaming profile: as a kid he booked; mid-1970s Las Vegas via Franzi; Cal Neva opener that Harrah's copied off his board; mixed Franzi's side number with Roxy's total. He is the living memory of how a shop actually took the other side of Walters / Banker / Franzi. Useful as market color, not as a model to copy.

Source: https://cdcgaming.com/commentary/faces-of-gaming-chris-andrews-dont-cry-for-the-bookmaker/

## Other names a serious source treats as NFL / US sharps

### Gadoon "Spanky" Kyrollos — CONFIRM (modern US operator)

Deutsche Bank → full-time sports, 2003. RJ, 5 Aug 2023: built page-scraping software (Spank Odds) to see discrepancies and steam; started by middling and scalping; later positions vs his number and chase-steam. Self-described operation: 14 full-time staff (injury, traders, tech, accountants), outsourced quants, many betting partners. Founded Bet Bash and the Sports Gambling Hall of Fame at Circa. Self-report to RJ of early $8,000 stakes growing past $1 million before they quit the bank — his number, not an audit. The useful fact is the org chart and the get-down problem ("the hard part is finding a bookmaker to accept your bets").

### Billy Baxter — CONFIRM (NFL halftimes; not a model shop)

Poker Hall of Fame and inaugural SGHOF. PokerNews, 2023: voters cited his advocacy and mastery of NFL halftime betting. Also Baxter v. United States, 633 F. Supp. 912 (D. Nev. 1986) — professional-gambler tax status. Roxy has called him his hero (RJ SGHOF story). No sourced lifetime NFL win rate here.

Source: https://www.pokernews.com/news/2023/10/billy-baxter-on-beating-irs-world-series-of-poker-44609.htm

### Jack Franzi ("Pittsburgh Jack") — CONFIRM (old-school wiseguy / oddsmaker)

Bettor first; oddsmaker at Barbary Coast and Gold Coast ~1979–1998. Died 2019, age 91. RJ: Art Manteris said Jimmy the Greek called Franzi for numbers before NFL Today segments. 1970 Pittsburgh bookmaking indictment thrown out — the judge found he was a big bettor, not a big bookie. Source: https://www.reviewjournal.com/sports/betting/bettor-oddsmaker-jack-franzi-dies-at-91-1691512/

### Charles K. McNeil — CONFIRM (invented the thing we bet)

University of Chicago master's; math teacher (Riverdale; students included JFK); securities analyst; then Chicago book. Credited with popularizing the football point spread ("wholesaling odds") in the early 1940s so both sides would bet. SI, 1986, called him "the wizard who gave the world the point spread" (via University of Chicago Magazine). Inaugural SGHOF. Not a modern syndicate.

Source: https://mag.uchicago.edu/economics-business/point-man

### Jimmy Evarts (Evart) — CONFIRM as Computer Group placement, not a solo legend

Named in period Computer Group reporting as a New York partner who placed a large share of the early wagers (with Stanley Tomchin) before Walters took over the move. 2024 SGHOF inductee, listed by RJ as an offshore bookmaker. Useful as a reminder that get-down was a job title.

Source: https://www.reviewjournal.com/sports/betting/what-sports-bettors-can-expect-to-get-out-of-bet-bash-4-at-circa-3118858/

Names looked at and not elevated: Haralabos Voulgaris (documented NBA / process, not NFL). Scott Schettler and Jimmy Vaccaro (books / Hall, other side). Jeff Whitelaw (named by RJ as a pro and SGHOF voter; no independent P&L in the sources used here).

## Ignore list (famous, not operators)

Use these as a negative screen. If a name is famous because of television, a 900 number, a Lamborghini, or a "lock," it does not go on the desk.

- Vegas Dave (Dave Oancea). Pick seller / influencer. Forbes: refused to produce a win-loss record; Showtime's Action showed losing customer bets; 2019 plea to a misdemeanor after a felony indictment over other people's Social Security numbers used to bet; sportsbook ban. Bill Krackomberger on Sports Handle: the car is from selling to "idiot kids," not from beating books.
- Action Network TV talent as a class. The site is a retail odds / public-betting dashboard. On-air "locks" are entertainment. Chad Millman et al. are media. Not a syndicate.
- Wayne Allyn Root and the rest of the 1990s–2000s infomercial / 900-number circuit. Same business as Vegas Dave, older wardrobe.
- Anonymous "Betting Professor" / "Picks with the Professor" / Professor MJ YouTube. Marketing handles. Not Bob Stoll, not Kent.
- Jimmy "The Greek" Snyder as a model for how to bet. See above.
- One-season Circa / SuperContest aliases with no other paper trail. They won a contest. That is all that is public.
- Social touts who advertise 70–80 percent. Stoll's own origin story (and every serious book) is that those ads are how the tout industry works.

Source for the tout distinction: https://sportshandle.com/sports-betting-pick-selling-paradox/

## What this desk actually copies

1. Make a number. Bet the residual, not the narrative. (Kent, Benter, Wong, Miller/Davidow)
2. Shop three-plus books. The edge is often the price, not the side. (Banker, Walters, Kyrollos)
3. Grade the book on closing-line value of $150 straights. Profit is variance. (Buchdahl, Pinnacle literature, this ledger's README)
4. Information that is not in the number (injury, rest, travel, weather) still matters; newspaper latency does not. (Banker then; Kyrollos injury desk now)
5. Get-down is a constraint. A correct number you cannot bet is a hobby. (Walters runners; Kyrollos on limits)
6. Ignore television. The Greek, Vegas Dave, and Action Network primetime are the public the line is shaded toward.
